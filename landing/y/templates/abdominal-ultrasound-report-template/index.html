ABDOMINOPELVIC ULTRASOUND
FICTIONAL EXAMPLE - NOT FOR CLINICAL USE
Illustrative content only; not a clinical reference.

Patient: FICTIONAL PATIENT
Identifier: DEMO-001
Date: 01 January 2026


Clinical indication: Intermittent abdominal discomfort in a fictional adult woman.

Liver: Smooth outline and homogeneous echotexture. No focal lesion seen.

Gallbladder: Well distended. No calculus or wall thickening seen.

Biliary tree: No intrahepatic duct dilatation. Common bile duct measures 4 mm.

Pancreas: Visualised portions appear unremarkable; tail partly obscured by bowel gas.

Spleen: Homogeneous echotexture; length 10.2 cm.

Right kidney: Length 10.8 cm. Corticomedullary differentiation maintained; no hydronephrosis.

Left kidney: Length 11.0 cm. Corticomedullary differentiation maintained; no hydronephrosis.

Urinary bladder: Adequately distended with a smooth wall; no intraluminal lesion seen.

Uterus: Anteverted, measuring 7.4 x 3.6 x 4.3 cm; homogeneous myometrium.

Endometrium: Measures 6 mm; correlate with menstrual history.

Right ovary / adnexa: Right ovary visualised; no adnexal mass seen.

Left ovary / adnexa: Left ovary visualised; no adnexal mass seen.

Prostate: Not applicable to this fictional patient.

Other findings / Peritoneum: No free intraperitoneal fluid seen.

Impression: No sonographic abnormality identified in the assessed structures. Limited visualisation of the pancreatic tail.

Recommendation: Correlate with the clinical assessment. This example demonstrates layout and does not prescribe investigation or treatment.

Clinician: DEMONSTRATION ONLY - NOT SIGNED

Using this reporting structure

Technique and organ findings

Describe the examination performed, relevant comparisons and limitations. Complete organ sections from the acquired examination; identify structures not visualised instead of leaving a reader to assume they were normal.

Measurements and impression

Check the displayed units before entering dimensions. Keep a focal finding’s location, size and appearance together. Summarise the principal observations and limitations in the impression, with clinician-authored recommendations where appropriate.

Documentation help, not clinical guidance. Use current specialty guidance and local protocols. Review every report before signing or sharing.
